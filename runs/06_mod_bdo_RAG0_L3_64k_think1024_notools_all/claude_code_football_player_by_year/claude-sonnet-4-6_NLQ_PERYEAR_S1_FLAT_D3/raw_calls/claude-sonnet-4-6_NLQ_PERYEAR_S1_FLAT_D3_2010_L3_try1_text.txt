## Steps and Citations in Order of Use

---

### Step 1 — Confirm the 2010 FIFA World Cup was held as scheduled

The 2010 FIFA World Cup was held in South Africa from 11 June to 11 July 2010. It was not cancelled or abandoned.

> **Citation 1:** Wikipedia, "2010 FIFA World Cup" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup

---

### Step 2 — Obtain the complete list of players named to national-team squads for the 2010 edition

Thirty-two nations each named a 23-player squad (736 players total). The definitive compiled list is on Wikipedia:

> **Citation 2:** Wikipedia, "2010 FIFA World Cup squads" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_squads

Key squad facts drawn from this source:
- **Argentina** squad (manager Maradona) included Lionel Messi.
- **Portugal** squad included Cristiano Ronaldo.
- **Brazil** squad included Kaká (selected despite injury concerns during the tournament).
- **Italy** squad (manager Lippi, defending champions) included Fabio Cannavaro as captain aged 36 — his farewell tournament.
- **England** squad included Michael Owen as a veteran forward option.
- **Spain** squad included Xavi Hernández and Andrés Iniesta as midfield starters.
- **France** squad (manager Domenech, the infamous 2010 campaign) included Karim Benzema and Franck Ribéry.
- **Croatia** did *not* qualify for 2010 (lost UEFA playoff to Ukraine), so Luka Modrić was absent.
- **Czech Republic** did not qualify, so Pavel Nedvěd was absent.
- **Sweden** did not qualify, so Zlatan Ibrahimović was absent.
- **Poland** did not qualify, so Robert Lewandowski was absent.
- Ronaldo Nazário, Ronaldinho, Rivaldo, Zidane, Figo — all either retired from international football or not selected before 2010.
- Neymar made his senior Brazil international debut in August 2010, *after* the tournament; he was not in the 2010 squad.

### Step 3 — Identify which of those squad players have ever won a Ballon d'Or or UEFA Men's Player of the Year / Best Player in Europe / Club Footballer of the Year

**Ballon d'Or winners source:**

> **Citation 3:** Wikipedia, "Ballon d'Or" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or

Relevant wins (year of award → player in 2010 squad):
- 2001 → **Michael Owen** (England squad ✓)
- 2006 → **Fabio Cannavaro** (Italy squad ✓)
- 2007 → **Kaká** (Brazil squad ✓)
- 2008 → **Cristiano Ronaldo** (Portugal squad ✓)
- 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2019, 2021, 2023 → **Lionel Messi** (Argentina squad ✓)
- 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017 → **Cristiano Ronaldo** (additional wins)
- 2022 → **Karim Benzema** (France squad ✓)

**UEFA Men's Player of the Year / previous names source:**

> **Citation 4:** Wikipedia, "UEFA Men's Player of the Year Award" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Men%27s_Player_of_the_Year_Award

The award has run continuously under different names:
- *UEFA Club Footballer of the Year* (from 1997–98 season onward)
- *UEFA Best Player in Europe Award* (from 2010–11 season, same award, renamed)
- *UEFA Men's Player of the Year* (later renaming, same lineage)

Relevant wins linking to 2010 squad members:
- 2006–07 → **Kaká** (Brazil ✓) — AC Milan Champions League winners
- 2007–08 → **Cristiano Ronaldo** (Portugal ✓) — Manchester United Champions League winners
- 2008–09 → **Xavi Hernández** (Spain ✓) — Barcelona treble winners
- 2010–11 → **Lionel Messi** (Argentina ✓) — Barcelona Champions League winners
- 2011–12 → **Andrés Iniesta** (Spain ✓) — outstanding performer at UEFA Euro 2012
- 2012–13 → **Franck Ribéry** (France ✓) — Bayern Munich treble winners
- 2013–14, 2015–16, 2016–17 → **Cristiano Ronaldo** (additional wins)
- 2014–15, 2018–19 → **Lionel Messi** (additional wins)

Players confirmed *not* to have won either award (and thus excluded despite being in 2010 squads): Wayne Rooney, Steven Gerrard, Wesley Sneijder, Arjen Robben, Robin van Persie, Fernando Torres, David Villa, Gianluigi Buffon, Andrea Pirlo, Didier Drogba, Samuel Eto'o, Luis Suárez, Diego Forlán, Thomas Müller, Mesut Özil, Thierry Henry, et al.
