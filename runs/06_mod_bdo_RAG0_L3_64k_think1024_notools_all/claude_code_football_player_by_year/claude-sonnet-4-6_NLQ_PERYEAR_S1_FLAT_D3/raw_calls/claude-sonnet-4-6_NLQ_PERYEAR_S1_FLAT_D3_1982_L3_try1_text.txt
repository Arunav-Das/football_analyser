## Steps and Citations (in order of use)

**Step 1 — Confirm the 1982 FIFA World Cup was actually held.**

[1] Wikipedia, "1982 FIFA World Cup," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup
Confirms the tournament was staged in Spain, 13 June – 11 July 1982, involving 24 national teams. The tournament was not cancelled or abandoned.

---

**Step 2 — Obtain the full list of players named to national-team squads for the tournament.**

[2] Wikipedia, "1982 FIFA World Cup squads," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_squads
Lists all 24 squads of 22 players each (528 players in total). Used to verify that Blokhin (USSR), Keegan (England), Rummenigge and Matthäus (West Germany), Rossi (Italy), and Platini (France) were all formally named. Key notes:
- **Oleg Blokhin** (USSR): included; he was 29 and still the USSR's key attacker.
- **Kevin Keegan** (England): included; played as late substitute in England's final group match v Spain despite a back injury.
- **Karl-Heinz Rummenigge** (West Germany): included; reached the final.
- **Paolo Rossi** (Italy): included; won the Golden Boot and Golden Ball.
- **Michel Platini** (France): included; France finished 4th.
- **Lothar Matthäus** (West Germany): included; debuted at senior international level in 1980 and was part of the 1982 squad.

No UEFA-award winner (UEFA Club Footballer of the Year first awarded 1998; UEFA Best Player in Europe from 2011) was active early enough to have been in a 1982 squad, so no UEFA-award row appears.

---

**Step 3 — Establish all Ballon d'Or winners and check overlap with the squad list.**

[3] Wikipedia, "Ballon d'Or," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or
Full historical list of winners consulted. Players confirmed to have won at least one Ballon d'Or AND to have appeared in Step 2's squad list:

| Player | Ballon d'Or year(s) | National team at 1982 WC |
|---|---|---|
| Oleg Blokhin | 1975 | USSR |
| Kevin Keegan | 1978, 1979 | England |
| Karl-Heinz Rummenigge | 1980, 1981 | West Germany |
| Paolo Rossi | 1982 | Italy |
| Michel Platini | 1983, 1984, 1985 | France |
| Lothar Matthäus | 1990 | West Germany |

Players considered and excluded: Diego Maradona (Argentina, in squad) — never formally awarded a Ballon d'Or (the award was restricted to European-passport players until 1995; the 2020 France Football "retroactive" exercise was not a formal award); Allan Simonsen (1977 winner) — Denmark did not qualify for 1982; Johan Cruyff (1971, 1973, 1974) — Netherlands did not qualify; Zbigniew Boniek (Poland, in squad) — finished 3rd in 1982 voting, did not win; Zico (Brazil, in squad) — never won the Ballon d'Or; Igor Belanov (1986 winner, USSR) — insufficient certainty he was in the 1982 USSR squad, omitted per instructions.

[4] Wikipedia, "UEFA Men's Player of the Year Award," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Men%27s_Player_of_the_Year_Award
Confirms award history (UEFA Club Footballer of the Year from 1998; renamed UEFA Best Player in Europe Award in 2011). All winners from 1998 onward were born after ~1972 and therefore could not have been named to any 1982 World Cup squad. No additional qualifiers.
