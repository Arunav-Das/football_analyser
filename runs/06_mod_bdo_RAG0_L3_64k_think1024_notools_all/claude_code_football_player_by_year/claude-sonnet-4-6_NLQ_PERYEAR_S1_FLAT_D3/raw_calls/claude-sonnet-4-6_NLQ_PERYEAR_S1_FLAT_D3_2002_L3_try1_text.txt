## Steps and Citations

**Step 1 — Confirm the 2002 FIFA World Cup was actually staged**

The 17th FIFA Men's World Cup was co-hosted by South Korea and Japan and ran from 31 May to 30 June 2002, producing a winner (Brazil). The tournament was not cancelled or abandoned.

> [1] Wikipedia, "2002 FIFA World Cup," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup

---

**Step 2 — Obtain the full list of players named to the 32 national-team squads**

All 32 squads, each comprising 23 players, are documented in the dedicated squads article. This was used to verify squad membership for every player discussed below.

> [2] Wikipedia, "2002 FIFA World Cup squads," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_squads

---

**Step 3 — Identify which of those squad members ever won the Ballon d'Or**

The Ballon d'Or (awarded annually by France Football since 1956) winners list was cross-referenced against the full squad list. The qualifying players and their winning year(s) are:

| Player | Nation at 2002 WC | Ballon d'Or year(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Zinedine Zidane | France | 1998 |
| Rivaldo | Brazil | 1999 |
| Luís Figo | Portugal | 2000 |
| Michael Owen | England | 2001 |
| Ronaldo (R9) | Brazil | 1997, 2002 |
| Fabio Cannavaro | Italy | 2006 |
| Ronaldinho | Brazil | 2005 |
| Kaká | Brazil | 2007 |

Notes on squad-membership edge cases:
- **Fabio Cannavaro** was an established Italian international by 2002 (28 years old) and was included in Italy's squad for the tournament. [2]
- **Kaká** (born 22 April 1982) made his Brazil senior debut in January 2002 and was selected as a 20-year-old reserve for Brazil's 2002 squad; he did not appear in any match but is confirmed as a squad member. [2]
- **Cristiano Ronaldo** (born 1985) and **Lionel Messi** (born 1987) were not part of any 2002 World Cup squad.
- **Pavel Nedvěd** (Ballon d'Or 2003) and **Andriy Shevchenko** (Ballon d'Or 2004) are not applicable because Czech Republic and Ukraine respectively did not qualify for the 2002 World Cup.

> [3] Wikipedia, "Ballon d'Or," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or

**Step 4 — Identify which squad members also won the UEFA Club Footballer of the Year (previous name of UEFA Men's Player of the Year)**

The UEFA Club Footballer of the Year was instituted in 1997–98. Among the 2002 World Cup squad members, the following are confirmed winners:

| Player | UEFA Club Footballer of the Year |
|---|---|
| Zinedine Zidane | 1997–98 (Juventus) |
| Ronaldinho | 2004–05 (Barcelona) |
| Kaká | 2006–07 (AC Milan) |

These three players thus appear in both columns (Ballon d'Or + UEFA award). The remaining five qualifying players qualified solely through the Ballon d'Or.
