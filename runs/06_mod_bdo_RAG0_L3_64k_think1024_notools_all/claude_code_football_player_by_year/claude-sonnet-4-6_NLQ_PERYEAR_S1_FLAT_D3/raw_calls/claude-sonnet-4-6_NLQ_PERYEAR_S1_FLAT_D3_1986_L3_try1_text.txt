## Steps and Citations

**Step 1 — Confirm the 1986 FIFA World Cup was actually held**

The 1986 FIFA Men's World Cup was held in Mexico from 31 May to 29 June 1986, completing its full run of matches. Twenty-four national teams participated; Argentina won the tournament, defeating West Germany 3–2 in the final.

[1] Wikipedia, "1986 FIFA World Cup" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup

---

**Step 2 — Obtain the full list of players named to national-team squads for the 1986 World Cup**

Each of the 24 participating nations named a squad of 22 players, giving 528 players in total. The main reference for all squad rosters is the dedicated Wikipedia article.

[2] Wikipedia, "1986 FIFA World Cup squads" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_squads

---

**Step 3 — Determine which of those players ever won the Ballon d'Or**

The France Football Ballon d'Or has been awarded annually since 1956. Until 1994 it was restricted to European-born players (or players with European citizenship); from 1995 it was opened to all nationalities. Cross-referencing the 1986 squad lists against the full list of Ballon d'Or laureates, four players qualify:

| Player | Country at 1986 WC | Ballon d'Or year(s) |
|--------|--------------------|---------------------|
| Michel Platini | France | 1983, 1984, 1985 (before the tournament) |
| Karl-Heinz Rummenigge | West Germany | 1980, 1981 (before the tournament) |
| Igor Belanov | USSR | 1986 (same year as the tournament) |
| Lothar Matthäus | West Germany | 1990 (after the tournament) |

Notes on players considered but excluded:
- **Diego Maradona** (Argentina): won the FIFA World Cup Golden Ball and the FIFA World Player of the Century vote but *never* won the France Football Ballon d'Or (Belanov won the 1986 edition; Maradona was runner-up).
- **Paolo Rossi** (Italy, Ballon d'Or 1982): Rossi retired from international football before the 1986 tournament; he was not named to Italy's 1986 squad.
- **Jean-Pierre Papin** (France, Ballon d'Or 1991): was not included in France's 1986 squad (he was just breaking through at Marseille).
- **Hristo Stoichkov** (Bulgaria, Ballon d'Or 1994): was not in Bulgaria's 1986 squad.
- **Ruud Gullit** (Ballon d'Or 1987) and **Marco van Basten** (1988, 1989, 1992): the Netherlands did not qualify for the 1986 World Cup.

[3] Wikipedia, "Ballon d'Or" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or

---

**Step 4 — Determine which of those players ever won a UEFA Men's Player of the Year / UEFA Club Footballer of the Year / UEFA Best Player in Europe Award**

The UEFA Club Footballer of the Year was first awarded for the 1997–98 season (won by Zinedine Zidane). Every subsequent winner (Rivaldo 1999, Figo 2000, Raúl 2001, Ronaldo 2002, Nedvěd 2003, Shevchenko 2004, Lampard 2005, Cannavaro 2006, Kaká 2007, C. Ronaldo 2008, Messi 2009 …) was born between 1972 and 1987 and therefore too young to have been named to any squad at the 1986 World Cup. No 1986 squad player was young enough to still be competing at elite level when the UEFA award began. Accordingly, **no additional players qualify through the UEFA route**.

[4] Wikipedia, "UEFA Club Footballer of the Year" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Club_Footballer_of_the_Year
